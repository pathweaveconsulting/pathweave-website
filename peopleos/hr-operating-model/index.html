PeopleOS · HR Operating Model

Clarify how people work is owned, delivered and governed.

As organisations grow, HR can become a collection of tasks rather than a clear operating model. PathWeave helps define what HR should own, what managers should own, how services should be delivered, where decisions sit and how the people function should scale with the business.

Common signs

  • Managers are unsure which decisions belong to HR
  • Routine issues escalate to founders or senior HR leaders
  • Different HR team members own overlapping work
  • HR is busy but service quality still varies
  • New locations or business units require repeated reinvention
  • Technology has been added without changing ownership or workflow

The design question

Who should do what, for whom, and with what authority?

An HR operating model translates people strategy into a practical delivery system. It defines the work HR performs, what managers and employees are expected to handle, how specialist support is accessed, and where decisions or exceptions are escalated.

The objective is not to create more hierarchy. It is to reduce ambiguity so recurring people work moves faster, similar situations receive similar treatment and senior HR capacity is reserved for the decisions that genuinely need it.

Where the model breaks

Growth usually exposes ownership problems before it exposes headcount problems.

HR owns too much

Managers send routine team decisions to HR because responsibility, confidence or escalation rules are unclear.

Managers own too little

Hiring, onboarding, feedback and performance are treated as HR processes rather than management responsibilities.

Work is person-dependent

Critical know-how sits with one experienced HR person, making quality and turnaround dependent on availability.

Services overlap

Internal HR, outsourced support, payroll, recruiters or specialist advisers touch the same process without clear boundaries.

Exceptions dominate

Policies and processes exist, but every case still requires senior judgement because decision rights are not explicit.

Technology does not solve ownership

An HRMS captures transactions but does not clarify who approves, decides, coaches, escalates or owns the outcome.

Operating-model components

What the model needs to make explicit

01

HR mandate

Which people outcomes HR is accountable for, which services it provides and where its role stops.

02

Manager responsibilities

What managers own in hiring, onboarding, performance, employee decisions and team operations.

03

Service delivery

How routine transactions, specialist advice and leadership support are delivered across the organisation.

04

Decision rights

Who can decide, approve, recommend or escalate people decisions, and when exceptions require senior review.

05

Governance rhythm

Forums, reviews, service measures and ownership that keep the people system operating and improving.

06

Capacity model

The right mix of internal HR, fractional leadership, outsourcing, technology and specialist support for the stage of growth.

A practical ownership model

Separate service delivery from people leadership.

HRDesigns frameworks, provides specialist advice, governs consistency, owns selected services and builds manager capability.
ManagersOwn day-to-day people leadership: selection decisions, onboarding quality, expectations, feedback, performance and team issues within the framework.
LeadershipOwns organisation choices, workforce priorities, critical talent decisions and the management standard expected across the business.
EmployeesUse defined self-service and manager routes for routine needs rather than depending on informal access to individual HR team members.
External partnersProvide defined specialist or transaction support with explicit service boundaries, data responsibilities and escalation routes.

Capacity choices

The right HR model changes with scale and complexity.

A growing SME may need a lean internal HR owner supported by Fractional HR, selected HR Outsourcing and clear manager ownership rather than a large HR department.

A larger or more complex organisation may need specialist roles, stronger service segmentation, clearer governance and workflow support through HR Operations & HRMS.

The question is not whether internal, outsourced or fractional HR is universally better. The question is which combination gives the organisation the required expertise, responsiveness, accountability and continuity at its current stage.

Design sequence

Start with work and ownership before drawing the HR organisation chart.

1. Map demandIdentify recurring HR services, specialist needs, manager questions, leadership support and transaction volumes.
2. Define ownershipDecide what sits with managers, internal HR, specialist roles, shared services or external partners.
3. Set decision rightsMake approval, exception and escalation logic explicit so routine work does not wait for senior people.
4. Design capacityTranslate work into the right mix of roles, external support, technology and management ownership.
5. Measure serviceUse a small set of service, quality and adoption measures to see whether the model is actually improving delivery.
