PeopleOS · Performance Management

Build a performance rhythm managers can actually run.

PathWeave helps organisations design performance management around clear expectations, regular manager conversations, evidence, calibration, development and follow-through — not just an annual appraisal cycle.

Start with an HR Health CheckDiscuss performance management

Common signs

  • Goals are set but rarely revisited during the year
  • Managers rate people differently for similar performance
  • Performance conversations happen only when something goes wrong
  • Employees are unclear about what good performance looks like
  • Development plans are written but not followed through
  • HR owns the process while managers treat it as administration

The operating principle

Performance management is a management system, not an HR form.

The system works only when managers can set expectations, observe performance, discuss evidence, make fair decisions and follow through consistently.

HR should design and govern the framework, but managers need to own the conversations. The process should be simple enough to use and strong enough to support pay, development, progression and accountability decisions.

Where systems usually fail

Most performance problems are operating problems, not form-design problems.

Goals are too broad

Employees receive general responsibilities rather than specific outcomes, measures or priorities they can act on.

Managers wait too long

Feedback is saved for a formal review, so course correction happens after the work or behaviour has already affected the team.

Evidence is inconsistent

Ratings depend on memory, visibility or manager style instead of a common expectation about what evidence matters.

Calibration is missing

Managers use the same rating scale differently, making cross-team decisions feel arbitrary even when intentions are good.

Development is disconnected

Development plans are created after reviews but are not tied to role expectations, next-level capability or manager follow-through.

HR owns the process

Managers complete forms because HR asks them to, rather than seeing performance as part of how they run their teams.

System components

What a practical performance system needs

01

Clear expectations

Role outcomes, goals, standards and behaviours translated into language employees and managers can use.

02

Manager check-ins

A realistic rhythm for feedback, prioritisation, support and course correction rather than one large annual conversation.

03

Evidence

Simple evidence sources that reduce memory bias and make performance discussions more concrete.

04

Calibration

Structured cross-manager review where necessary to improve consistency and reduce rating drift.

05

Development

Specific development priorities connected to the role and next-level capability, with ownership and follow-through.

06

Consequences and recognition

Clear links to recognition, progression, corrective action and other people decisions without turning the system into a scoring exercise.

Manager ownership

Define what HR designs and what managers must do.

HRDesigns the framework, tools, governance, communication and consistency checks.
ManagersSet expectations, observe, coach, document evidence, discuss performance and follow through on actions.
LeadershipSets the standard for accountability and ensures performance decisions reflect business priorities and organisational values.
EmployeesUnderstand expectations, bring evidence, ask for support and own agreed actions and development commitments.

Design sequence

Start with roles and management behaviour before choosing ratings.

1. Clarify rolesDefine the outcomes and responsibilities that performance discussions should actually evaluate.
2. Define expectationsTranslate business priorities into role-relevant goals, standards and behavioural expectations.
3. Set the rhythmChoose a realistic cadence for check-ins, formal reviews and calibration based on the organisation's management capacity.
4. Equip managersProvide simple tools and guidance for feedback, evidence, difficult conversations and development discussions.
5. Connect decisionsClarify how performance information informs recognition, progression, development and corrective action without pretending one score makes every decision.

Different stages need different systems

A 40-person SME should not copy a 4,000-person appraisal process.

Smaller organisations often need fewer forms and more manager discipline: clear expectations, monthly or quarterly conversations and a simple record of actions. As the organisation grows, calibration, role levels, structured development and stronger governance may become necessary.

The same principle applies to startups, technology teams, multi-location organisations and GCCs: the system should reflect role complexity, management maturity and decision needs, not a generic corporate template.
