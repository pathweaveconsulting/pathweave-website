The SME reality

What worked at 20 people often stops working at 80 or 150.

Early-stage businesses can operate on founder judgement, direct communication and flexible roles. Growth changes that equation. There are more managers, more employee decisions, more handoffs and more variation in how work gets done. Informal HR begins creating delays, inconsistency and dependence on a few people.

The answer is not to copy a large-company HR model. SMEs need enough structure to make decisions repeatable while keeping the organisation practical and fast.

PathWeave SME HR growth-stage framework

Build only the HR capability the next stage genuinely needs.

These employee ranges are practical guides, not legal thresholds. Business complexity, workforce type, locations and hiring pace can move the need earlier or later.

STAGE 1

1–20 · Foundations

HR ownership: usually founder or nominated administrator. Priorities: clear offers and joining, essential policies, basic employee records, role clarity and consistent hiring. Technology: keep it simple; do not buy an HRMS to compensate for undefined processes.

STAGE 2

20–50 · Process consistency

HR ownership: clearer internal ownership or external support. Priorities: repeatable recruitment, onboarding, leave and employee-change workflows, manager basics and a simple organisation structure. Technology: begin defining data and workflow requirements.

STAGE 3

50–100 · HR ownership + managers

HR ownership: a dedicated HR role or more deliberate fractional/outsourced model. Priorities: manager capability, performance rhythm, role clarity, workforce planning and stronger recruitment governance. Technology: HRMS may now add value if workflows are clear.

STAGE 4

100–200 · Operating model + governance

HR ownership: a clearer HR team structure and service model. Priorities: decision rights, performance governance, manager expectations, people reporting and scalable employee lifecycle. Technology: HRMS, workflow automation and useful management information become more important.

STAGE 5

200+ · Specialist capability

HR ownership: stronger internal specialisation with defined external support where useful. Priorities: organisation effectiveness, talent and capability, leadership pipeline, workforce analytics and HR transformation where legacy processes no longer fit.

ALWAYS

Manager capability grows with the system

At every stage, HR becomes more effective when managers progressively own hiring decisions, onboarding quality, expectations, feedback, performance and team-level people decisions rather than delegating them back to HR.

How much HR capability should an SME build?

Internal, fractional, outsourced or hybrid depends on the work—not fashion.

Internal HR makes sense when the organisation has enough recurring work, manager demand and business context to justify dedicated capacity. Fractional HR can add experienced leadership when a full-time senior hire would be premature. HR outsourcing can provide dependable delivery for defined recurring processes. A hybrid model often works when an internal owner needs external specialist or execution capacity.

The decision should consider continuity, expertise, responsiveness, manager access, data ownership and the amount of work that genuinely needs to sit inside the company.

Questions SME founders ask

Practical decisions, not HR theory.

When should we hire our first HR manager?

There is no single headcount threshold. The signal is usually recurring manager demand, hiring volume, employee-lifecycle complexity and founder time. The role should be designed around the work the business actually needs.

Do we need an HRMS?

Only when the employee data and workflows are clear enough for a system to improve them. Define requirements first; software should support the operating model, not become the operating model.

Should HR sit with the founder?

In a very small business, often temporarily. As the organisation grows, routine people decisions should progressively move to managers and a defined HR owner so the founder retains only the decisions that truly require leadership judgement.

Should we outsource HR?

Outsource clearly defined work where external delivery improves capability or continuity. Keep internal ownership of business context, leadership decisions and the people outcomes the organisation cannot delegate.

When should performance management become formal?

When manager inconsistency, growth in team size or unclear expectations start affecting execution. Formal does not need to mean bureaucratic; a simple recurring rhythm is often enough at first.

How do we know what to fix first?

Start with the point causing the most business friction—ownership, hiring, manager capability, performance, employee lifecycle or HR data—then sequence the remaining work around that priority.
